\*Betweenness was estimated with a sampled‑node approximation (k = 500).  

**Degree Centrality** measures the proportion of possible ties a node actually has. For an undirected graph  

\[
C_D(v)=\frac{deg(v)}{N-1}
\]

where *deg(v)* is the number of neighbours of *v* and *N* is the number of nodes.  

**Betweenness Centrality** captures how often a node lies on shortest paths between other pairs of nodes. For node *v*  

\[
C_B(v)=\sum_{s\neq v\neq t}\frac{\sigma_{st}(v)}{\sigma_{st}}
\]

where \(\sigma_{st}\) is the total number of shortest paths from *s* to *t*, and \(\sigma_{st}(v)\) those that pass through *v*. The reported values are approximations based on 500 sampled source nodes.  

**Closeness Centrality** reflects how close a node is, on average, to all other nodes in the network; higher values indicate shorter average distances.

### 7. Influence Diffusion Simulation  

#### 7.1 Model description  

The **Independent Cascade (IC) model** is a classic stochastic diffusion framework. The process starts from a **seed** node; each newly activated node gets a single chance to activate each of its inactive neighbours with a fixed **spread probability** *p*. The simulation proceeds in discrete rounds until no further activations occur or a pre‑set round limit is reached.

#### 7.2 Configuration used  

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Seed user (anonymous ID) | **107** |
| Spread probability *p* | **20 %** (0.20) |
| Maximum rounds | **5** |
| Random seed for reproducibility | **42** |
| Network size | **4 039** users |

#### 7.3 Results (probabilistic scenario)  

The simulation produced the following round‑by‑round counts (the numbers are expected values under the random seed, not guarantees of real Facebook sharing):

| Round | Newly influenced | Cumulative influenced |
|-------|------------------|-----------------------|
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 193 | 194 |
| 2 | 870 | 1 064 |
| 3 | 1 055 | 2 119 |
| 4 | 675 | 2 794 |
| 5 | 258 | **3 052** |

- **Total influenced users:** 3 052  
- **Network reach:** 75.56 % of the sampled graph  

These figures illustrate a plausible diffusion trajectory under the chosen probability and round limit. They should be interpreted as **probabilistic scenarios** generated by the IC model, not as predictions of actual Facebook behaviour.

### 8. AI‑Based Recommendations  

1. **Study Highly Connected Users** – Users with the largest degree centralities (e.g., 107, 1684, 1912) act as hubs. Analysing their activity patterns can reveal common topics or behaviours that attract many contacts.  

2. **Examine Bridge Positions** – Users with high betweenness and many cross‑community edges (especially 107, 1912, 1684) are strategic for spreading information across community boundaries. Targeted messaging to these bridges can accelerate inter‑community diffusion.  

3. **Leverage Community Leaders** – Each community’s leader (see Table 3) can serve as a local champion. Engaging them may improve acceptance of messages within their group while limiting spill‑over to unrelated groups.  

4. **Validate Structural Influence** – The Enhanced Influence Score reflects only network topology. Before drawing conclusions about real‑world popularity, compare these scores with behavioural data (likes, comments, shares) to confirm that structural prominence translates to actual engagement.  

5. **Analyse Cross‑Community Edges** – Quantify how many edges exist between each pair of communities. This information helps identify which community pairs are most tightly linked and where bridging interventions could be most effective.
